## 1. Phase Objective

Phase 3A.6 established Potato as READY WITH LIMITATIONS and Tomato as NOT READY for full-window forecasting. This phase does not re-argue that decision — it takes Potato as given and answers nine concrete questions (target variable, horizon, information availability, leakage prevention, train/test strategy, evaluation metrics, baselines, and permissible claims) that must be settled in writing before a single feature is built. Getting these wrong first and fixing them after training would risk quietly building leakage or an indefensible evaluation into the eventual model; this phase exists to prevent that.

## 4. Target Variable Decision

Three candidate price fields were compared quantitatively, not by default convention:

| Field | Mean | Stdev | Coefficient of variation | Mean abs. day-over-day change | Distinct values (of 178) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `min_price_rs_quintal` | 1409.8 | 284.1 | **0.202** (highest) | **290.4** (highest) | 49 |
| `max_price_rs_quintal` | 2018.1 | 161.4 | 0.080 (lowest) | 131.7 (lowest) | 35 |
| `modal_price_rs_quintal` | 1752.2 | 182.4 | 0.104 (middle) | 186.1 (middle) | 44 |

**Decision: `modal_price_rs_quintal` is selected as the primary forecasting target.**

**Justification, addressing each required dimension:**
- **Economic meaning:** modal price is Agmarknet's own headline figure — the price at which the largest transacted volume actually cleared that day. It represents what a typical transaction actually costs, which is the figure most relevant to a vendor's real procurement decision. Minimum and maximum price represent the best-case and worst-case extremes of that day's trading range, not what most transactions actually looked like.
- **Data availability:** identical to the other two candidates — populated on all 178 existing rows, no missing values within present rows.
- **Usefulness to the platform:** this project's own prior wholesale-price figures (Phase 1B/1C) were already drawn from modal price, so using it here keeps this component consistent with figures already reported elsewhere in the project, rather than introducing a second, differently-defined "price" concept.
- **Stability:** modal price is not the most stable field (max price is, at CV 0.080) — but it is also not the noisiest (min price is, at CV 0.202, with 56% higher day-over-day volatility than modal). It sits in between, which is the right trade-off: a target that is too stable (max price) risks being an uninformative, ceiling-driven number; a target that is too volatile (min price) risks being dominated by single-lot outlier trades rather than the market's actual center.
- **Research interpretability:** modal price is the figure most directly comparable to published Agmarknet reporting conventions and to this project's own existing figures, making any result easier to sanity-check against outside sources.

**Explicitly not chosen automatically:** the task's own instruction not to default to modal price without justification is honored above by actually comparing all three fields' statistics before selecting — modal price wins on the combination of economic relevance and moderate (not extreme) volatility, not because it is the "usual" choice.

**Secondary targets — future extensions only, not part of this experiment:** `min_price_rs_quintal` and `max_price_rs_quintal` could later support a price-range or volatility-band forecast (e.g., predicting the spread rather than a single point), but this is named here only as a possible future direction and is explicitly out of scope for the experiment this phase defines.

## 5. Forecasting Horizon Definition

**Verified fact, not assumption:** the gap between *consecutive observations* (not consecutive calendar dates) was measured directly:

| Gap between consecutive observations | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 1 calendar day (true next-day) | 168 | 94.9% |
| 2 calendar days (one date skipped) | 9 | 5.1% |
| 3+ calendar days | 0 | 0% |

This confirms the distinction the task requires: **"next calendar day" and "next available observation" are not the same thing in this dataset**, though they coincide 94.9% of the time. There is no case in this dataset where more than one calendar day was skipped between two consecutive observations (consistent with Phase 3A.6's finding that every Potato gap is a single isolated day).

**Decision: the forecasting horizon is defined as one-step-ahead over available observations — predicting the next *recorded* observation, not the calendar-next day unconditionally.**

**Why this framing, not "next calendar day":** defining the task as "predict tomorrow's price" would silently assume tomorrow has a price to predict in 5.1% of cases where it structurally does not (a missing date). Framing the target as "the next available observation, whatever its actual calendar distance" is the only framing that is honest about what the dataset can actually supply on every single training/evaluation instance, without requiring an assumption about an unobserved date.

**Multi-day (e.g., 3-day) horizons were considered and not adopted for this phase's experimental scope:** Phase 3A.1 originally proposed a 3-day trend-classification horizon for a different, richer anticipated dataset. With only 178 observations and a real (if small) rate of 2-day observation gaps, a fixed multi-day-ahead regression target would need to decide, for each of the 9 gap-adjacent instances, whether "3 days ahead" means 3 calendar days or 3 observations ahead — another leakage/definition risk this phase would rather avoid than paper over. One-step-ahead-by-observation is proposed as the defensible starting scope; a multi-step extension is left as future work, not attempted here.

**Formal problem definition:**

Let observations be indexed by their position in the chronologically-ordered, already-existing dataset (not by calendar date): $t = 1, 2, \dots, 178$, where $t$ corresponds to the $t$-th real, present observation, and $d_t$ is that observation's actual calendar date (with $d_{t+1} - d_t \in \{1, 2\}$ days, verified above, never larger).

$$y_t = \text{modal\_price\_rs\_quintal at observation } t$$

The forecasting task is:

$$\hat{y}_{t+1} = f\big(y_1, \dots, y_t,\; d_1, \dots, d_t\big)$$

i.e., predict the next *recorded* modal price using only observations up to and including index $t$. The calendar date $d_{t+1}$ is not assumed to be $d_t + 1$; it is whatever date the next real observation actually falls on, and that date itself only becomes known information once the observation exists (Section 7). No notation implying a fixed daily grid (e.g., $y_{t+1}$ meaning "tomorrow") is used, because that would misstate what the data can support.

## 6. Information Availability Analysis

**A. Information available before predicting observation $t+1$ (usable as features):**
- All price values ($y_1, \dots, y_t$) and their calendar dates ($d_1, \dots, d_t$) up to and including the most recent available observation.
- Derived quantities computed *only* from that same history (e.g., a lag value, a moving average over the last $k$ observations, the elapsed-day count since the previous observation) — provided they are computed strictly from data at or before index $t$.
- Calendar-derived features of $d_t$ itself (e.g., day-of-week, day-of-month) — these are knowable in advance and do not leak anything.

**B. Information only known after the target date (must NOT be used as a feature for predicting it):**
- The target value itself, $y_{t+1}$ (the modal price being predicted).
- $y_{t+1}$'s own min/max price on the *same* observation — these are reported together in the same source row and only become known once that day's trading has concluded and been reported; using `min_price_rs_quintal` or `max_price_rs_quintal` *from the target date* as a feature for that same date's modal price would be same-day leakage, since none of the three fields is available before the others for a given date.
- Any statistic (mean, rolling window, normalization scale) computed using data from index $t+1$ or later.

**Consequence for future feature engineering (not performed in this phase):** every feature must be constructed using a rule of the form "known strictly before the target observation," and specifically, the target date's own `min_price_rs_quintal`/`max_price_rs_quintal` must never be used to predict that same date's `modal_price_rs_quintal` — all three fields are reported simultaneously in the source, not sequentially.

## 7. Data Leakage Prevention Policy

Explicit rules, to be enforced when feature engineering eventually begins:

| Concern | Rule |
|---|---|
| Future prices | No feature for predicting $y_{t+1}$ may use $y_{t+1}, y_{t+2}, \dots$ — only $y_1, \dots, y_t$. |
| Future dates | $d_{t+1}$ is never treated as "known in advance to equal $d_t+1$"; any elapsed-time feature must be computed from the actual, already-realized gap up to $t$, never assuming the next date's value ahead of time. |
| Same-target-date variables | `min_price_rs_quintal` and `max_price_rs_quintal` on the row being predicted must never be used as features for that row's `modal_price_rs_quintal` (Section 6). |
| Rolling statistics | Any rolling mean/median/stdev must be computed using a strictly trailing, closed-on-the-left window ending at or before index $t$ — never a centered or forward-looking window. |
| Lag features | Lag-$k$ features must be indexed by *observation position*, not calendar offset, given the confirmed existence of 2-day observation gaps (Section 5) — a naive "yesterday's price" feature implemented via calendar-date lookup would silently produce a missing/undefined value on 9 of 178 rows, which is a bug risk this rule exists to flag in advance, not a leakage risk, but is recorded here since it is exactly the kind of subtle scripting error a leakage-focused review should catch alongside real leakage. |
| Scaling/normalization | Any scaler (e.g., min-max, z-score) must be fit only on the training partition (Section 8) and applied unchanged to validation/test data — never fit on the full dataset before splitting. |
| Train/test preprocessing | Any preprocessing step with parameters learned from data (imputation statistics, scaling parameters, encodings) must be fit on the training split only and applied forward; this dataset currently requires no imputation (Section 2), so this rule is preventive/future-facing rather than something this phase had to apply today. |

## 8. Train / Validation / Test Strategy

**Random shuffling is explicitly rejected**, per the task's own instruction and this project's standing rule (Phase 3A.1 onward): shuffling 178 chronologically-ordered observations before splitting would let a model trained on later data "see" patterns from a future it should not yet know about relative to an earlier held-out point — this is textbook temporal leakage for a series this short and would invalidate any reported metric.

**Options evaluated:**
- **Chronological holdout** (a single split: earliest $n$ observations train, most recent $m$ observations test): simplest, but with only 178 observations, one fixed split risks the test window landing on an unrepresentative stretch (e.g., a short-term price spike) purely by chance, with no way to check if that happened.
- **Expanding-window validation** (train on an initially-small window, evaluate on the next few observations, then grow the training window and repeat): makes better use of a small dataset by generating multiple evaluation points instead of one, and never re-orders data.
- **Walk-forward validation** (a specific, fixed-size-or-expanding rolling variant of the above, re-fitting at each step): the most rigorous option, but on 178 observations, refitting at every single step is computationally trivial (this is not a data-volume concern) — the real question is how many folds are meaningful given the small sample.

**Recommendation: expanding-window (walk-forward) validation, with a final held-out chronological test tail — not a single arbitrary holdout.**

Proposed (not executed in this phase) structure:
- An initial training window of roughly the first 60% of observations (~107 of 178) to allow enough history for any lag/rolling feature to be computed before the first evaluation point.
- Successive expanding-window folds over the next ~20% (~36 observations), each fold adding the previously-evaluated points back into training before evaluating the next stretch — this produces several evaluation points instead of one, which matters given how few observations exist.
- A final, never-touched-during-tuning chronological tail of the most recent ~20% (~35 observations) reserved as the test set, used only once, after all modeling/validation decisions are finalized.

**This 60/20/20-style split is stated explicitly as a proposed design choice requiring validation once real feature engineering begins, not a formally derived optimum** — with 178 total observations, any split ratio is a judgment call, and this one is chosen to leave a genuinely usable amount of history in the initial training window while still reserving a meaningful, multi-week final test tail. **No split was actually executed in this phase** — this section defines the strategy, per the task's instruction, without performing it.

## 9. Baseline Models

**A simple, non-ML baseline is mandatory, not optional**, for one concrete reason specific to this dataset: with only 178 observations and a target this volatile (Section 4), it would be easy for a complex model to appear to "work" while actually doing no better than trivially repeating the last known price. Without a baseline, that failure mode is invisible.

**Required baselines for this experiment:**
1. **Persistence / naive forecast:** $\hat{y}_{t+1} = y_t$ — predict that the next observation equals the last known one. This is a classically strong baseline for daily commodity prices precisely because prices are serially correlated day-to-day; a real model must beat this, not merely differ from it.
2. **Moving average:** $\hat{y}_{t+1} = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=t-k+1}^{t} y_i$ for a small window $k$ (e.g., 5–7 observations) — a mildly smoothed alternative to pure persistence, useful for checking whether a model is doing better than simple smoothing alone.

**No complex ML model is selected in this phase** — per the task's explicit instruction, model selection is deferred to a later phase; this section only fixes the floor every future model must clear.

## 10. Evaluation Metrics

| Metric | Appropriate here? | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **MAE** (Mean Absolute Error) | ✅ **Primary metric** | Directly interpretable in ₹/quintal — the same unit as the target — and not disproportionately sensitive to the occasional large price swing already observed (mean_abs day-over-day delta of 186.1 for modal price, Section 4). |
| **RMSE** (Root Mean Squared Error) | ✅ **Secondary metric** | Reported alongside MAE specifically to surface whether a small number of large errors is driving overall performance (RMSE penalizes large errors more than MAE) — useful given modal price's observed volatility, but not adopted as primary because it is less directly interpretable in plain ₹ terms on its own. |
| **MAPE** (Mean Absolute Percentage Error) | 🔴 **Checked and rejected as a primary metric** | Modal price in this dataset ranges from ₹1,300 to ₹2,300/quintal (Section 4) — it never approaches zero, so MAPE would not blow up numerically here the way Phase 3A.1 flagged as a general risk for glut-period vegetable prices. However, MAPE is still not adopted as primary: it weights percentage error, which would make a ₹100 error at a ₹1,300 price look worse than the same ₹100 error at a ₹2,300 price, and this project has no stated reason to prefer that weighting over plain ₹/quintal error. It may be reported as a supplementary, easy-to-communicate figure, but MAE remains primary. |

**Interpretation rule, stated explicitly:** lower MAE/RMSE means the model's predicted modal price is, on average, closer in ₹/quintal terms to the actual next observed modal price. A model is only reported as an improvement if its MAE is lower than **both** required baselines' MAE (Section 9) — beating one baseline but not the other is not sufficient to claim success.

## 11. Forecast Reliability and Abstention Concept

Per Phase 3A.6's recommendation, Tomato's 66-day blackout (2025-06-04 to 2025-08-08) is used here as the motivating, concrete example for a conceptual system rule — **not implemented or coded in this phase**, only defined:

**Proposed abstention rule (design concept, requiring future validation — not a formally derived statistical threshold):**

> Before producing a forecast for a given commodity/geography series, the system should check the *recent* data continuity leading up to the prediction point. If the most recent required lookback window contains a gap exceeding some threshold number of consecutive missing days, the system should **abstain** from forecasting (or issue an explicit low-confidence warning) rather than silently produce a number from a model trained on data unrelated to current conditions.

**Motivating evidence:** Tomato's Segment A (2025-03-05 to 2025-06-03, 82 observations) and Segment B (2025-08-09 to 2025-08-31, 17 observations) are separated by a 66-day gap. A naive system that ignored this and trained "one Tomato model" on both segments concatenated would be implicitly forecasting August prices from a model that last saw real data in early June — 66 days of unknown market movement (a monsoon-affected period, by calendar timing, though this report does not confirm that as the cause) would have occurred with the model having no visibility into it. This is exactly the situation an abstention rule exists to catch.

**Explicitly labeled as proposed, not derived:** the specific numbers below are illustrative starting points for a future phase to validate, not conclusions this audit has proven optimal:
- A candidate consecutive-gap threshold of "greater than ~14 days" as a trigger for abstention is offered as a *starting point for discussion*, loosely anchored to Phase 3A.1's own 3-day forecast horizon (a gap several times longer than the horizon itself plausibly invalidates any lag-based feature) — **this number is not validated in this phase and must not be treated as settled.**
- Tomato's own gap (66 days) would trigger abstention under almost any non-trivial threshold choice, which is precisely why it is the motivating example rather than a borderline case requiring careful threshold-tuning to classify correctly.

## 13. Limitations and Non-Claims

This experimental definition, and any model eventually built from it, does **not**:
- Predict vendor demand — no demand-side data exists in this dataset (no arrival/quantity field; Section 3).
- Represent individual street vendors — it describes an aggregate district-level price signal, not any specific vendor's purchase price or experience.
- Represent Bowenpally mandi-level Potato prices — the data is Hyderabad district-level, a proxy only (Section 2, restated deliberately).
- Guarantee future market prices — any forecast is a statistical estimate with quantified historical error (Section 10), not a promise; the abstention concept (Section 11) exists precisely because this project does not claim its data can support a forecast under all conditions.
- Use Tomato as an equal forecasting experiment — Tomato is excluded from this experimental scope per Phase 3A.6's readiness finding and is reserved for the abstention-motivating role described in Section 11, not a parallel model.
- Merge Tomato and Potato into any shared table, feature set, or comparative claim — they remain two separate, differently-scoped, non-comparable series (Phase 3A.5/3A.6, unchanged).

**Additional limitations specific to this phase's own analysis:**
- The 60/20/20-style split proposed in Section 8 and the ~14-day abstention threshold proposed in Section 11 are both explicitly flagged as unvalidated design choices, not derived optima — a future phase should treat them as starting points to test, not settled parameters.
- All statistics in this report describe the *existing* 178 observations; nothing here characterizes or predicts behavior during the 9 missing dates or any date outside the 2025-03-05–2025-09-07 window.

## 14. Recommendation for the Next Phase

Phase 3A.8 (or equivalent) should proceed to **feature engineering scoped exactly as defined in Section 12** — building lag/rolling/calendar features indexed by observation position (Section 7's leakage rule), fitting any scaler only on the training partition, and preparing the expanding-window fold structure proposed in Section 8 — still without training a final model. Model training itself should be deferred to the phase after that, so the feature-engineering step can be reviewed against this phase's leakage rules (Section 7) before any model sees the data.
