# Phase 3A.6 — Dataset Readiness and Experimental Scope Audit

**Date of audit:** 2026-09-13
**Scope:** Determine, on evidence alone, which of the two Phase 3A.5 processed datasets (Tomato/Bowenpally, Potato/Hyderabad-district-proxy) are ready for ML experimentation, what missing-data policy is defensible, and what experimental scope should carry forward. No model is trained in this phase.
**Rule followed throughout:** no raw or processed file from a prior phase was modified. No missing date was filled, interpolated, or bridged. Every number below comes from running `scripts/phase3a6_dataset_readiness.py` against the actual Phase 3A.5 outputs.
**Reproducibility:** every result in this report can be regenerated by running `scripts/phase3a6_dataset_readiness.py` from the project root. It only reads `data/processed/tomato_bowenpally_daily.csv` and `data/processed/potato_hyderabad_daily.csv`, and writes exactly one new file (Section 10).

---

## 1. Phase Objective

Phase 3A.5 delivered two validated but structurally very different datasets. This phase does not re-validate their internal cleanliness (already done) — it asks a narrower question: **are either of them, as they currently stand, ready to be carried into feature engineering and ML modeling, and if not, what is the scientifically honest thing to do instead?** This phase produces a decision, not a model.

## 3. Dataset Readiness Criteria

Defined here, before evaluating either dataset, so the criteria are not fitted to whatever the data turns out to show:

| # | Criterion | What "pass" means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Temporal ordering | Dates strictly non-decreasing when read in file order |
| 2 | Duplicate observations | Zero dates appearing more than once |
| 3 | Data coverage | Fraction of the 187-day study window (2025-03-05 to 2025-09-07) with a real observation |
| 4 | Consecutive missing periods | Length of the single worst contiguous gap, not just the total missing count |
| 5 | Dataset size | Total usable observations, and per-segment size if the series is broken by a large gap |
| 6 | Geographic consistency | Every record maps to one single, correctly-labeled geography (no silent level-mixing) |
| 7 | Target variable availability | A price field usable as a forecasting/classification target exists and is populated whenever a row exists |
| 8 | Unit consistency | Price/quantity units are stated or defensibly inferred, and consistent across the file |
| 9 | Feature availability | Fields beyond the bare price exist that a future model could use (e.g., arrival quantity, variety) |
| 10 | Suitability for chronological train/test splitting | A large enough *contiguous* span exists to hold out a trailing test window without the split itself straddling a large gap |

## 5. Tomato Contiguous Segment Analysis

The two segments named in this phase's scope, evaluated directly:

| Segment | Range | Calendar days | Observations | Missing within segment | Density | Meets project's 90-day floor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Segment A** | 2025-03-05 to 2025-06-03 | 91 | **82** | 9 | 90.1% | ✅ Yes (91 ≥ 90) |
| **Segment B** | 2025-08-09 to 2025-08-31 | 23 | **17** | 6 | 73.9% | ❌ No (23 < 90) |

The 90-day floor used above is not a new number invented for this phase — it is Phase 3A.3's own previously-established minimum ("at least 90 continuous days for a first usable model; 6–12 months preferred," Phase 3A.3 Section 3). Reusing it keeps this judgment consistent with what the project already committed to.

**Segment A: borderline, not comfortably sufficient.** It clears the 90-calendar-day floor by exactly one day, but that floor was originally stated for a *continuous* series — Segment A is not fully continuous; it carries 9 internal single-day gaps of its own (90.1% density). Compared to Potato's full 178-observation, 95.2%-dense series over the same 187-day window, Segment A is both shorter and less dense. It is large enough to *attempt* a small chronological train/test split (e.g., train on the first ~65 days, test on a trailing ~2–3 week window), but any result from such a split would rest on a genuinely small sample and should not be presented as comparably strong evidence to a Potato-based result.

**Segment B: insufficient, not borderline.** 23 calendar days and 17 real observations is well short of even a minimal single-month clean window, let alone the 90-day floor. It is not treated as a candidate for any standalone experiment in this report.

**Segments are not combined.** Per this phase's explicit instruction, Segment A and Segment B are never concatenated into one continuous series — doing so would silently manufacture a 187-day-looking dataset out of two disjoint 3-month and 3-week windows separated by a 66-day hole, which would misrepresent what was actually observed. They are reported and evaluated strictly as two separate, disjoint candidates.

**Conclusion on Tomato forecasting suitability:** Tomato, as a single 187-day series, is **not suitable for a scientifically meaningful continuous-horizon forecasting or trend-classification experiment**. Segment A alone is a marginal candidate for a much smaller-scope experiment, but even that carries a caveat this report is not willing to paper over: a model trained and tested entirely within Segment A would be evaluated only on March–June behavior and would say nothing about the vendor's actual, currently-relevant August/September price environment, since none of that period survived the blackout with usable density (Segment B is too short, and September has zero data). Given this, **Tomato is better characterized in this project as a data-quality limitation case than as a forecasting dataset** (Section 11).

## 7. Potato Missing-Gap Analysis

| # | Missing date | Gap length | Single-day or multi-day |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2025-03-30 | 1 day | Single-day |
| 2 | 2025-04-27 | 1 day | Single-day |
| 3 | 2025-05-01 | 1 day | Single-day (coincides with a national holiday — Labour Day) |
| 4 | 2025-05-27 | 1 day | Single-day |
| 5 | 2025-06-22 | 1 day | Single-day |
| 6 | 2025-06-25 | 1 day | Single-day |
| 7 | 2025-07-21 | 1 day | Single-day |
| 8 | 2025-07-24 | 1 day | Single-day |
| 9 | 2025-08-23 | 1 day | Single-day |

**Is preprocessing justified?** Evaluating the three options this phase's scope names:

- **Option A — leave missing, use only available chronological records.** No transformation risk, no assumption introduced. The cost is that a model consuming this data must itself tolerate irregular spacing (e.g., lag features computed on "the previous *available* day" rather than "yesterday").
- **Option B — documented interpolation for small isolated gaps only.** Technically defensible *in principle* for single-day gaps (all 9 qualify as "small and isolated"), but interpolation still injects an assumption (typically linearity) about a value that was never observed, and this project's own standing rule (Phase 3A.2 onward) is not to manufacture data points even when the manufacturing method is simple and well-intentioned. For a research paper, an interpolated value is defensible only if the paper is explicit that it is a modeled estimate, not a reported price — that framing adds complexity Potato's very small gap count (4.8%) does not need.
- **Option C — regular daily index with missing values retained (explicit flag) until model-specific handling.** Zero fabrication, and it gives a future feature-engineering step a ready-made, unambiguous signal for "this date has no real observation" without having to re-derive the missing-date list from scratch.

**Decision: Option A is the primary recommended policy — leave the 9 dates absent, use only the 178 real records.** This is the most defensible choice for a research paper specifically because Potato's missing-data rate (4.8%) is small enough that dropping/skipping those 9 dates costs almost nothing in sample size, and it avoids introducing any interpolation assumption that would need its own justification and disclosure. **Option C is additionally provided, not as a replacement for Option A but as a convenience artifact** (Section 10) — a regular 187-row daily index with the 9 missing dates present as empty price fields plus an explicit `is_missing=1` flag, so a future modeling step can choose its own handling (e.g., skip, mask, or a documented model-specific imputation) without redoing this phase's gap analysis. **Option B (interpolation) is explicitly not adopted** — it was not chosen "merely to maximize dataset size," consistent with this phase's instruction, since Option A already leaves 178 of 187 days usable.

## 8. Geographic Granularity Limitations

Restated plainly, because this phase's instructions treat getting this wrong as a serious integrity failure, not a minor imprecision:

- **Tomato is Bowenpally APMC, a single named mandi** — genuine market-level data, matching this project's original Phase 1B/1C study-site scope.
- **Potato is Hyderabad district-level data, used as a geographic proxy.** It is an average across however many mandis within Hyderabad district reported that day (evidenced by its non-integer price values) — it is **not** Bowenpally data, **not** Gaddiannaram data, and this report does not describe it as either.
- **The two datasets do not share geographic granularity.** They cannot be merged into one table, compared row-for-row as if measuring the same location, or used to imply that "Hyderabad district trended up" therefore "Bowenpally trended up" (or vice versa). This limitation was already established in Phase 3A.5 (Section 8/9 there) and is carried forward unchanged, not re-litigated.

## 10. Recommended Missing-Data Policy

**Potato: Option A (leave the 9 missing dates absent; use the 178 real records as-is) is the primary policy**, for the reasons in Section 7. As a documented convenience, not a replacement, this phase additionally generated:

**`data/processed/potato_hyderabad_daily_regular_index.csv`** — a new file, never overwriting the Phase 3A.5 output, containing all 187 calendar-day rows for the study window. The 178 real dates carry their actual `min_price_rs_quintal`/`max_price_rs_quintal`/`modal_price_rs_quintal` values unchanged; the 9 missing dates carry **empty** price fields and an explicit `is_missing=1` flag. No price value was invented for any row. This view exists purely so a future feature-engineering step does not have to re-derive which dates are missing — it is a bookkeeping convenience, not a cleaned or "complete" dataset, and it must not be described as one.

**Tomato: no filled, interpolated, or index-regularized version was created, and none is recommended.** Per this phase's explicit gap policy, the 66-day blackout is not bridged in any form — not by interpolation, not by an empty-flagged regular index, not by anything else. Tomato's only defensible further step, if pursued at all, is the raw Segment A subset described in Section 5, used and reported strictly as a 91-day partial window, never re-labeled as "the Tomato series."

## 11. Recommended Experimental Scope

Evaluated against the three options this phase names:

- **Option A — Potato becomes the primary forecasting experiment.** ✅ **Adopted.** Potato is the only dataset that passes the chronological-split-suitability criterion cleanly, has near-complete coverage, and has zero multi-day gaps. This is not a "settle for what's easy" choice — it is the dataset that actually satisfies Phase 3A.1's own stated requirements (Section 3 there: daily granularity, chronological validity, sufficient span) that Tomato does not currently satisfy for the full window.
- **Option B — Tomato becomes a secondary limited experiment.** 🟡 **Not adopted as a parallel forecasting track**, but not fully discarded either: Segment A (Section 5) remains available as a small, explicitly-scoped exploratory check (e.g., "does a persistence baseline behave similarly on Bowenpally market-level data as on Hyderabad district-level data, within the one 91-day window where Bowenpally has near-complete coverage?") — but this must be reported as a small, secondary, non-primary check, never presented with the same confidence as a Potato-based result, and never combined with Segment B or the post-blackout period.
- **Option C — Tomato is retained only as a data-quality failure/abstention case.** ✅ **Adopted as Tomato's primary framing going forward.** Tomato's 66-day structural blackout is a genuine, well-evidenced example of exactly the situation a responsible system should recognize and refuse to forecast through, rather than silently paper over. This project's methodology (Phase 1B onward: verify before trusting, report failures honestly) is better served by presenting Tomato as **a demonstrated case for a data-availability guardrail** — a system that checks a series' recent continuity before producing any forecast, and abstains or issues an explicit low-confidence warning when a request would require forecasting across or immediately after a blackout like this one — than by forcing a full Tomato model to exist for its own sake.

**Recommended scope carried into feature engineering / ML modeling:**
1. **Primary and only full-scope ML dataset: Potato (Hyderabad district-level), using `potato_hyderabad_daily.csv` as the record of truth and `potato_hyderabad_daily_regular_index.csv` as an optional bookkeeping aid** — every future output must state "Hyderabad district-level," never "Bowenpally."
2. **Tomato is not carried forward as a forecasting dataset for the full window.** Segment A may be used, if at all, only as a small, clearly-labeled secondary/exploratory check — never as an equal-footing second commodity experiment, and never merged with Potato.
3. **Tomato's blackout is documented and reused as the project's concrete justification for a data-availability abstention rule** in the eventual system design — this is a real research contribution (a system that knows when *not* to forecast), not a consolation prize for a commodity that "didn't work out."
4. Onion remains out of scope, unchanged from Phase 3A.2–3A.4's findings.

## 12. Explicit Non-Claims and Limitations

This report does **not** claim:
- That Potato data represents Bowenpally or Gaddiannaram mandi prices — it represents Hyderabad district, a proxy only.
- That Tomato and Potato have the same geographic granularity — they explicitly do not (Section 8).
- That Segment A's 82 observations constitute a strong or publication-grade sample — it is described throughout as marginal/borderline, clearing a day-count floor while falling short of the density and continuity a stronger experiment would want.
- That interpolating Potato's 9 missing dates would be wrong in every conceivable research context — only that, for this project's current stated preference for a research-paper-defensible approach, leaving them absent (Option A) is the stronger choice over inventing values (Option B).
- That the new regular-index Potato file is a "cleaned" or "final" dataset — it is a bookkeeping convenience with explicit missing-value flags, not a preprocessing result that changes what is known.
- That any model has been trained, tuned, or evaluated in this phase — none has.

**Limitations this phase could not resolve:**
- The cause of Tomato's 66-day blackout remains unknown (Phase 3A.5, Section 13) — this phase inherits that uncertainty rather than resolving it.
- Potato's price units remain inferred (₹/quintal by convention), not source-confirmed.
- The 90-calendar-day floor used to judge Segment A (Section 5) is itself a reused prior-phase heuristic, not a formally derived statistical power calculation — Segment A's "borderline" status should be read as a qualitative judgment, not a precise statistical guarantee of adequacy or inadequacy.

## 13. Recommendation for the Next Phase

Phase 3A.7 (or equivalent) should proceed to **feature engineering scoped to Potato only** (Hyderabad district-level, price fields, using `potato_hyderabad_daily.csv` as the primary source and the regular-index file as an optional aid for gap-aware feature construction), explicitly deferring any Tomato modeling. In parallel, the system-design track should incorporate the data-availability abstention concept motivated by Tomato's blackout (Section 11) as a named design requirement, not merely a footnote — this is the most scientifically honest way to keep Tomato relevant to the project without pretending its data problem has been solved.
